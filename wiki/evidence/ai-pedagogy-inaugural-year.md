# Evidence: AI Pedagogy Specialist — Inaugural-Year Reach

**Provenance:** [VALIDATED] — reported in a peer-reviewed paper (2026 ASEE, Best Overall
Paper Finalist). Aggregate institutional metrics; no individuals identified.

## Activity & reach (2025–2026 inaugural year)
- **Needs-assessment surveys:** **155 faculty/instructors** (~18% of the instructional
  community) and **107 students**.
- **Programming reach:** **69 faculty/staff** across four sessions —
  - AI Pedagogy Lab 1, *Ethics & Academic Integrity* (Oct 2025): 18 participants.
  - AI Pedagogy Lab 2, *Assignment & Assessment Redesign* (Nov 2025): 8 participants.
  - AI Pedagogy Lab 3, *AI Policy Dissemination* (Mar 2026): 14 participants.
  - Food for Thought 1, *Navigating AI Together* (Feb 2026): 29 participants.
- **Faculty AI policy survey:** **43 policies** collected and analyzed across five
  disciplinary groupings.
- **Individual faculty consultations** on assignment redesign, AI expectations,
  responding to suspected misuse, and tool selection.
- **Infrastructure:** the AI Pedagogy website ([aipedagogy.wpi.edu](https://aipedagogy.wpi.edu)),
  a faculty listserv,
  an **AI Advisory Circle** (8 definite + 11 possible faculty), **two student
  ambassadors**, and an **Assignment Redesign Worksheet**.

## Notable needs-assessment findings
- **58%** of faculty use AI mainly for their **own work, not teaching**.
- **Ethics & academic integrity** was the top faculty support need (**56%**).
- **Clear AI policies** was the top student need (**50%**).
- **60%** of submitted faculty AI policies had **no enforcement mechanism**.

## Why it counts as evidence
Concrete, peer-reviewed evidence that the inaugural AI Pedagogy Specialist role reached a
substantial share of the instructional community and produced actionable institutional
findings — the strongest validation to date for the [[ai-pedagogy]] thread.
