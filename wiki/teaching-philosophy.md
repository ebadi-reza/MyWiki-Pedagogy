# Teaching Philosophy — The Three Pillars

This is the spine of the wiki. Reza's teaching philosophy is stated as three pillars,
and nearly all of his engineering-education work is an expression of one or more of
them. Every thread, practice, tool, and source page links back here.

Provenance legend: **[CLAIM]** = Reza's own assertion · **[VALIDATED]** = backed by
external evidence · **[ASPIRATIONAL]** = future/pending.

## Origin of the philosophy

[CLAIM] The philosophy took shape in response to two large shifts in higher education —
the COVID-19 pandemic and the arrival of generative AI. The pandemic altered how
students consume material and affected student mental health; AI is changing students'
relationship to knowledge and their critical-thinking habits. The three pillars are
Reza's deliberate response to both. *(Sources: [[candidate-statement]], [[covid-statement]].)*

## The three pillars

### 1. Accessibility — [[accessibility]]
[CLAIM] Making robust engineering content available outside the classroom, and
providing cost-effective alternatives to expensive tools and equipment — so that
students are not pushed toward unverified or erroneous free resources. Channels include
recorded lecture videos (with viewing metrics tracked), CAD tutorial videos, lecture
**note templates**, open educational resources, low-cost lab hardware, and AI-powered
tools as access instruments.

### 2. Stress reduction — [[stress-reduction]]
[CLAIM] Reducing unnecessary course and exam anxiety **while maintaining rigor** — on
the premise that anxiety consumes the cognitive bandwidth learning requires. The stated
aim is to "remove the learning obstacles, not the challenge." This is the most mature
pillar: piloted in Heat Transfer (A22), expanded to Kinematics and Statics, with survey
data collected at each stage and an external evaluation. See [[stress-reduction]].

### 3. Self-sufficiency — [[self-sufficiency]]
[CLAIM] Developing students who leave with the judgment and skills to solve unfamiliar
problems — by building confidence in their own reasoning, exposing them to
professional tools and methods, and teaching them to evaluate resources critically.
Reza's approach to generative AI is an extension of this pillar: neither uncritical use
nor outright refusal, but building the judgment to know when AI is right, when it is
wrong, and what to do about it. See [[self-sufficiency]].

## Quality vs. impact

Reza's own dossier separates two dimensions of teaching that this wiki keeps distinct:

- **Teaching quality** — how content is delivered: consistently strong course/instructor
  evaluations, live problem-solving and live-demo practices, and iterative course
  development. See the evidence in [[teaching-evaluations]].
- **Teaching impact** — lasting effects beyond the individual course, organized around
  the three pillars above. Recognized externally by the [[morgan-worcester-instructorship]].
