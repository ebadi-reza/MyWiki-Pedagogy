# AI Verification Cycle

Teaching practice serving the [[self-sufficiency]] pillar; the classroom seed of the
future [[ai-pedagogy]] research program.

## What it is
[CLAIM] A structured **weekly** cycle introduced in **Kinematics (ME 3310) beginning
C26**. The goal is judgment: knowing **when AI is right, when it is wrong, and what to do
about it** — AI as a tool checked against one's own reasoning, not a substitute for it.

## The weekly cadence
[CLAIM] As presented at the 2026 AI Summit ([[2026-ebadi-ai-summit]]), the week runs on a
fixed rhythm:
- **Thursday (−6 days) — TA testing:** a TA runs the week's problem through an AI model.
- **Monday (−2 days) — TA report:** the TA reports what the AI produced.
- **Tuesday (−1 day) — student quiz:** students solve the problem **independently** first.
- **Wednesday — AI Lab:** the instructor solves it **live**, then the class **evaluates
  the AI's response together in real time**, using **PMKS+** to manipulate the linkage and
  trace curves interactively.

**Grading: full credit for participation, not correctness** — which lowers the stakes,
reduces anxiety, and creates space for honest engagement (an explicit crossover with
[[stress-reduction]]).

## The teaching moment (worked example)
[CLAIM] In a Kinematics coupler-curve problem, ChatGPT was **fluent but not fully
correct**: it computed the rocker length and the rotation period correctly, but got the
**coupler length wrong** — assuming the straight segment spanned ~60–70° of crank rotation
instead of **counting the dashes** on the plot. The lesson, in Reza's framing: *the AI
sounds right; only domain knowledge reveals whether it is.* Students then classified the
AI's output (correct-and-trustworthy / correct-but-not-trustworthy / outright wrong).

## Framing / takeaways
[CLAIM] The stated principles:
- **Confidence ≠ Correctness** — AI outputs are fluent by design.
- **Expertise cannot be outsourced** — verification requires subject knowledge; the aim is
  graduates who **reason alongside AI, not defer to it**.
- **Simple, scalable, transferable** — the framework adapts to any discipline where domain
  knowledge enables critical evaluation.

## Design principle: space for objection
[CLAIM] Participation is **optional** for students with principled objections to AI use.
One student (anonymized) declined on ethical grounds regarding how AI models are trained
and run; Reza respected the decision, framing space for disagreement as part of
responsible pedagogy.

## Precursor evidence
[VALIDATED] In Eng. Exp., students used AI for coding only after demonstrating they
understood the underlying algorithm; comprehension was verified on an **offline
midterm**, on which students performed well — indicating AI served as an implementation
tool, not a substitute for understanding. See [[self-sufficiency]].

## Research trajectory
[ASPIRATIONAL] Intended as the first step toward a coherent pedagogical framework on **AI
verification and judgment**, with Reza's courses as the laboratory and [[gpthermo]] /
[[studyguideai]] as research platforms. A **"Summer Sandbox" proposal** would formalize
the approach in Kinematics as weekly **"AI dissection"** sessions (the [[teaching-portfolio]]
uses that phrasing for the same practice line). See [[ai-pedagogy]].
