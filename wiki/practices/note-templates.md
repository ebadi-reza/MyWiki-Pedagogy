# Lecture Note Templates

Teaching practice serving the [[accessibility]] pillar. Also a clear example of
[[iterative-course-development]] — the design emerged from several offerings.

## What it is
[CLAIM] Distributed lecture note templates that leave **targeted blanks** for students
to fill in critical details during lecture — a middle path between passive slides
(low-retention) and full note-taking (tedious, distracting from engagement).

## How it evolved (Kinematics)
[CLAIM] The template was the third answer to a problem Reza kept refining:
- **C23** — required students to take notes during class (note-taking supports learning),
  but this burdened some students.
- **C24** — published his complete lecture notes, so students could focus on content;
  but full notes were counterproductive for others, who assumed they could rely on posted
  notes/videos later and often did not follow through.
- **C25** — designed the **fill-in template** with targeted spaces, promoting active
  note-taking without mindless transcription. Feedback was overwhelmingly positive, and
  he adopted the approach in **Statics (B25)**.

## Evidence
[VALIDATED] On the 2024 IEC survey (Kinematics, n = 47), **47%** of students rated the
fillable lecture notes **highly or extremely helpful**. See [[2024-ebadi-iec]] /
[[stress-reduction-survey-results]].

[CLAIM] Student feedback otherwise reported as overwhelmingly positive. *(A sample of the
template is Appendix A.7 of the [[teaching-portfolio]].)*
