# Kinematics Community-Mechanism Project

A project-based, service-oriented practice in **Kinematics of Mechanisms (ME 3310)**,
serving the [[self-sufficiency]] pillar (open-ended problem solving) with a communication
and service-learning dimension. Adopted and then refined from a WPI colleague's course
(co-author in [[glossary]]).

## What it is
[CLAIM] Student teams **design, analyze, and fabricate a simple mechanism to help a
community in need**, and must **explain the working principles in plain language to that
non-technical audience**. The emphasis is on solving an **open-ended, real-world**
problem and communicating it — not a pre-specified answer.

Example projects: a low-cost water-filter mechanism, a can crusher, a pill puncher, a pet
feeder, a corn sheller.

## Why it serves the pillars
[CLAIM] It requires students to define and solve an unfamiliar problem (self-sufficiency),
work in a larger team, and translate technical work for a lay audience — one of Reza's
project-based "high-impact practices."

## Evidence
[VALIDATED] In the 2023 study, **38.6%** of surveyed students agreed the open-ended
project helped them understand the topic — a figure the authors attribute to **survey
timing** (administered right after the midterm, before teams seriously engage the
project). See [[2023-ebadi-asee]] and [[stress-reduction-survey-results]].
