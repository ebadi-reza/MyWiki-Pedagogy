# Midterm Make-Up Exams

Assessment practice serving the [[stress-reduction]] pillar. Rethinks **what happens
after a poor performance** so a bad midterm becomes a structured recovery, not a fixed
penalty.

## How it works
[CLAIM] The midterm covers four topics. After grading, the instructor identifies **each
student's lowest-scoring topic**. That student may then earn a retake by either:
1. **Re-studying** the topic independently (e.g. over a weekend), or
2. **Teaching** the topic to someone else — a friend, family member, or even an
   imaginary audience — and **recording a video** of the lesson for credit.

The teaching option is deliberately open-ended: students have used whiteboard
explanations, screen-sharing, interactive discussion, simulations, and more, matching
the method to their own learning style.

## Evidence
[VALIDATED]
- **40.8%** average improvement in exam performance (SD **29.4%**); in the 2023 study,
  **14 of 29** students (≈48%) opted in, and **all but one improved** on the make-up
  (scores ranged 12→68 out of 100). See [[2023-ebadi-asee]].
- **48%** of eligible students chose the "teach it" option.
- **65.4%** agreed that teaching their area of weakness enhanced their comprehension.
- **65.3%** reported a better experience in Kinematics than in similar courses; **76.9%**
  would want other professors to adopt similar modules.

Figures above are from the Kinematics survey (n = 26, ~90% response) in the 2023 ASEE
paper. A later survey (2024 IEC, n = 47) found **62%** rated the make-up exam **highly or
extremely helpful**. See [[stress-reduction-survey-results]].

## Where/when it ran
Piloted in the stress-reduction set; run in Kinematics (C23, C24, C25) and Statics (B24).
