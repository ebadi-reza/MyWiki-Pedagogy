# Source: 2024 IEC — "Combating Exam Stress" (poster)

**Citation:** R. Ebadi (as A. Ebadi), "Combating Exam Stress: Implementing Peer
Discussions to Enhance Learning and Reduce Anxiety," invited presentation/poster,
*2024 Innovative Education Conference* (University of Rhode Island, virtual).

## What it presents
A one-poster synthesis of the [[stress-reduction]] practices with a **new survey** — a
**Junior-level ME course (Kinematics), n = 47, 100% participation** — distinct from the
2023 ASEE study ([[2023-ebadi-asee]], n = 26). It reports three things: how anxious
students were, how helpful each module was, and self-reported learning gains.

## Key aggregate findings
*(Percentages combine the top two response categories, as labeled, and are read from the
poster's charts.)*

**Module helpfulness** — rated **highly or extremely helpful**:
- [[weekly-group-quizzes]]: **83%** (61.7% "extremely")
- [[midterm-makeup-exams]]: **62%**
- [[note-templates]] (fillable lecture notes): **47%**
- [[two-part-final-exams]] (the "Redo exam"): **38%**

**Self-reported learning gains** — **good or great gain**:
- Confidence doing kinematics analysis: **68%**
- Enthusiasm for engineering: **66%**
- Confidence understanding the material: **60%**
- Interest in taking more engineering classes: **53%**
- Comfort taking a quiz or exam: **28%**

**Anxiety prevalence (motivation)** — reported **highly or always true**:
- "After an exam, I worry whether I did well enough": **55%**
- "During important exams, I think I'm doing awful or may fail": **23%**
- "I worry so much before a major exam I'm too worn out to do my best": **19%**

## Notes
This is a conference **poster/presentation**, not a full paper — aggregate figures only;
no individual data. It is the invited **Innovative Education Conference** talk noted in
the [[cv]] and [[scholarship-and-dissemination]]. Figures recorded on
