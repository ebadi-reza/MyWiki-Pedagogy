# Source: 2026 AI Summit — "Real-Time AI Verification: Teaching Critical Evaluation with Domain Expertise"

**Citation:** R. Ebadi, "Real-Time AI Verification: Teaching Critical Evaluation with
Domain Expertise," *2026 AI Summit*, Worcester, MA (invited presentation). *(The deck
states the pedagogical framework is the author's own; only visual design was AI-assisted.)*

## What it presents
The definitive walkthrough of the **[[ai-verification-cycle]]** — Reza's own framework for
teaching students to **verify** AI answers with domain expertise, not merely generate
them. A companion peer-reviewed presentation, "A Weekly Cycle for AI Verification," was
given at the 2026 Capital PKAL Regional Network ([[scholarship-and-dissemination]]).

## The argument
- **The Why** [CLAIM]: we have always taught skills beyond domain knowledge (writing,
  teamwork, entrepreneurial thinking); teaching **ethical and effective AI use** is now
  part of that. Prompt engineering was the first wave and is passing; AI outputs are now
  fluent and increasingly trusted. **The skill that matters is using domain expertise to
  verify AI answers.**
- **The weekly process** [CLAIM]: TA testing (Thu) → TA report (Mon) → student quiz (Tue)
  → **AI Lab** (Wed), with **full credit for participation, not correctness**. Detailed on
  [[ai-verification-cycle]].
- **The demo** [CLAIM]: the instructor solves a Kinematics coupler-curve problem live,
  then the class evaluates the AI's response together using **PMKS+** interactively.
- **The teaching moment** [CLAIM]: the AI was fluent but got the coupler length wrong (it
  assumed a crank-rotation range instead of counting dashes), while getting the rocker
  length and period right — "the AI *sounds* right; domain knowledge reveals whether it
  *is*."

## Takeaways
[CLAIM] **Confidence ≠ Correctness**; **expertise cannot be outsourced** (reason alongside
AI, don't defer to it); the framework is **simple, scalable, and transferable** to any
domain-knowledge discipline; and **participation over correctness** lowers stakes and
leaves room for students who opt out entirely.
