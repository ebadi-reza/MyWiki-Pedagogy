# Source: 2024 ASEE — "Capstone Projects for Self-Efficacy, Skills, and Successful Careers"

**Citation:** "Capstone Projects for Self-Efficacy, Skills, and Successful Careers,"
*2024 ASEE Annual Conference & Exposition*, Portland, OR (Paper ID #41526). A
**multi-author WPI study led by a WPI colleague**; **R. Ebadi (as A. Ebadi) is a
co-author**, not the lead. *(Co-authors in [[glossary]]; the lead author is de-identified
by the wiki's publication policy.)*

## What it is (and Reza's role)
This is a large quantitative **alumni study** — the lead author's engineering-education
research program — that Reza **contributed to as a co-author**. It is included here as
part of his publication record and because it studies, at the institutional level, the
kind of capstone/MQP work Reza does as an advisor ([[project-advising]]). The findings
below belong to the study, not to Reza individually.

## Study design
- **Sample:** ~**2,101** WPI bachelor's alumni (graduated 1980–2019), from a March 2021
  alumni survey (emailed to 15,528).
- **Method:** quasi-experimental; hierarchical multiple linear regression, controlling
  for demographics and career-satisfaction response bias (p < .05).
- **Constructs:** three professional-skill composites (communication, information use,
  teamwork), **self-efficacy**, and **career preparedness**.

## Headline findings
- [VALIDATED] Capstone/MQP experiences have a **moderate, significant positive effect** on
  developing communication, information-use, and teamwork skills **and self-efficacy** —
  even after controls; higher **capstone quality** produces meaningfully larger gains.
- Gains in professional skills (esp. communication and information use) **and** gains in
  self-efficacy each **independently predict career preparedness**.
- **Self-efficacy largely mediates** professional skills' effect on career preparedness:
  once self-efficacy is accounted for, ~**79%** of the variance previously attributed to
  professional skills is also explained by self-efficacy.

## Why it connects to Reza's work
Self-efficacy — the belief one can tackle a task successfully — is the research-literature
counterpart to the [[self-sufficiency]] pillar ("confidence to solve problems you have not
seen before"). The study's subject, **WPI capstone projects (MQPs/IQPs)**, is exactly the
advising Reza does; see [[project-advising]]. Background note: the required MQP/IQP
structure originates in the **WPI Plan** (adopted 1970). See [[glossary]].
