# Source: 2025 Global School Forum — "Project-Based Learning for the Age of AI"

**Citation:** R. Ebadi, "Project-Based Learning for the Age of AI: Challenges and
Opportunities in Engineering Education," *2025 WPI Global School Forum*, Worcester, MA
(presentation). *(The deck discloses it was AI-assisted and fully presenter-reviewed and
verified — consistent with Reza's verification ethos.)*

## What it argues (a position/framework talk, not new data)
- **PBL's promise** [CLAIM]: critical thinking, teamwork/collaboration, real-world
  application.
- **PBL's reality in engineering** [CLAIM]: within tight 7-week terms, instructors face
  time constraints, risk aversion, and limited per-student support, so projects stay
  rudimentary.
- **AI as a barrier-remover** [CLAIM]: an always-available first point of contact that
  handles tedious calculation, debugging, and initial troubleshooting — letting
  instructors **raise the bar** to more complex, multifaceted projects.
- **Case study — ME 3902 Engineering Experimentation** [CLAIM]: shifting from pre-tested
  sensors + provided code to **unfamiliar sensors integrated with AI**, moving the
  learning focus from syntax to experimental design and interpretation. (Aligns with the
  validated offline-comprehension evidence in [[self-sufficiency]].)

## The critical risk: uneven AI readiness (an equity/access point)
[CLAIM] Assuming all students can use AI effectively is wrong — some distrust it, others
lack the metacognitive skills to prompt well or critically evaluate outputs. Raising
project complexity **without universal AI fluency creates inequity**, leaving struggling
students further behind. This ties the AI-readiness gap to the [[accessibility]] pillar.

## Timeline & recommendations
- **Near future (through 2029)** [ASPIRATIONAL]: bridge the AI-literacy gap — clear AI-use
  policy, discuss policy and learning objectives, prompt-engineering training, integrate
  AI with scaffolding, support hesitant students.
- **Distant future (2030+)** [ASPIRATIONAL]: AI as a ubiquitous tool; students arrive
  fluent; courses become multidisciplinary and industry-complex; education refocuses on
  higher-order thinking.
- **Five faculty recommendations** [CLAIM]: (1) start small, scale thoughtfully;
  (2) **teach AI as a tool, not a crutch** (prompting, output validation, when *not* to
  rely on AI); (3) redesign assessments around engineering reasoning, **solution
  verification**, experimental design, and critical thinking; (4) support hesitant
  students; (5) build faculty capacity through professional development.
