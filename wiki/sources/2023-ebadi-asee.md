# Source: 2023 ASEE — "Learning Engineering Concepts Through Teaching It"

**Citation:** R. Ebadi (as A. Ebadi) & a WPI colleague, "Learning Engineering Concepts
Through Teaching It," *2023 ASEE Annual Conference & Exposition*, Baltimore, MD
(Paper ID #38232). Peer-reviewed. *(Co-author in [[glossary]].)*

## Thesis (the paper's own frame)
The paper's lens is **learning through teaching** — not, in its own words, "stress
reduction." It hypothesizes that having students **teach** a concept (to a friend,
classmate, family member, or imaginary student) bridges the gap between intuitive and
principled understanding, improving comprehension and retention. This is the evidence
base for the *comprehension* benefit of the practices that the [[teaching-portfolio]] and
[[candidate-statement]] later frame under [[stress-reduction]].

## Study design
Conducted in **Kinematics of Mechanisms** at a mid-sized technological university, via
three modules — note these differ from the "three stress-reduction practices" grouping:
1. **[[weekly-group-quizzes]]** — 30-min paired quiz + 20-min instructor debrief; full
   credit for participation regardless of correctness.
2. **[[kinematics-community-project]]** — an open-ended term group project designing a
   simple mechanism to help a community, explained to a non-technical audience.
3. **[[midterm-makeup-exams]]** — students re-teach their lowest-scoring topic (recorded
   video) and take a make-up on that topic.

Effectiveness measured via **grades** (midterm vs. make-up) and an instructor-designed
**self-evaluation survey**.

## Results
**Grades:** 14 of 29 students (≈48%) opted to teach their weakest topic and retake a
make-up; make-up scores ranged 12→68/100; **average improvement 40.8% (SD 29.4%)**, with
all but one student improving.

**Survey** (n = **26**, 90% of the class) — "agree" (somewhat + strongly):
| Q | Item | Agree |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | Weekly quizzes helped me learn the topic | **77%** |
| Q2 | "Sometimes learned from / sometimes taught" my partner | **65.4%** |
| Q3 | Teaching my area of weakness helped me understand | **65.4%** |
| Q4 | The open-ended project helped me understand | **38.6%** |
| Q5 | Better experience than in similar courses | **65.3%** |
| Q6 | Would want other professors to adopt these modules | **76.9%** |

The authors attribute Q4's lower figure to **survey timing** (administered right after
the midterm, before students engage the project). These figures are recorded on
[[stress-reduction-survey-results]].

## Honest limitations (stated by the authors)
No control group; survey administered early (small project sample); exam-retake adds
student workload; peer-teaching can risk overconfidence. Recommended future work:
end-of-term survey and a control group.
