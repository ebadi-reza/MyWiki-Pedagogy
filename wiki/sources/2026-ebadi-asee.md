# Source: 2026 ASEE — "Supporting Faculty in the AI Era"

**Citation:** R. Ebadi *et al.* (co-authors incl. J. Hill; one co-author de-identified by
policy — see [[glossary]]), "Supporting Faculty in the AI Era: Lessons from a New AI
Pedagogy Role," *2026 ASEE Annual Conference & Exposition* (Paper ID #51473).
Peer-reviewed; **Reza is lead author**. *(Title variant: the paper's running header reads
"…Lessons from a New AI Pedagogy Specialist Initiative.")*

**Recognition:** [VALIDATED] **Finalist, Best Overall Paper, Faculty Development Division,
2026 ASEE.**

## What it documents
The inaugural year of WPI's **AI Pedagogy Specialist** role (2025–2026) within the MTLC —
the definitive account of the role Reza holds. It reads the role through the **POD
Network's "Defining What Matters"** framework, in which centers for teaching and learning
serve four functions — **hub, incubator, temple, sieve** — and shows the new role
extending three of them (temple/recognition de-emphasized in year one):
- **Sieve** — filtering and promoting best practices (the AI Pedagogy website, a faculty
  listserv, research-grounded programming).
- **Incubator** — coaching faculty to redesign assignments/assessments (an Assignment
  Redesign Worksheet), roughly doubling the center's redesign-support capacity.
- **Hub** — convening early and late adopters; an AI Advisory Circle.

## Needs assessment (Fall 2025) — key findings [VALIDATED]
**Faculty survey (155 respondents, ~18% of the instructional community):**
- **58%** use AI mainly for **their own work, not student-facing**; only 26% for
  classroom activities, 26% for prep, 17% for student-facing resources; 12% opposed to AI.
- Skill (n=117): 39% beginner, 45% intermediate, 9% advanced, 6% expert.
- Top desired support: **ethics & academic integrity (56%)**, classroom AI activities
  (47%), assignment/assessment redesign (44%).

**Student survey (107 respondents, mostly engineering/CS):**
- Heavier use (≈45% frequent); but **34% opposed to AI in their academic work** (higher
  than faculty).
- Top need: **clear AI policies from instructors (50%)**.

## Implementation & inaugural-year reach [VALIDATED]
See the evidence page [[ai-pedagogy-inaugural-year]]. In brief: two needs-assessment
surveys (155 faculty, 107 students); **three AI Pedagogy Labs** (Ethics & Academic
Integrity, Oct 2025; Assignment Redesign, Nov 2025; AI Policy Dissemination, Mar 2026);
one **Food for Thought** session (Feb 2026); a **faculty AI policy survey (43 policies)**;
an **Assignment Redesign Worksheet**; individual faculty consultations; an AI Advisory Circle
and two student ambassadors — **reaching 69 faculty/staff** across the four programming
sessions.

## Faculty AI policy landscape (43 policies) [VALIDATED]
Five philosophies: **tiered-by-assignment 49%**, full prohibition 19%, disclosure-first
14%, permissive-with-guardrails 9%, pedagogically-integrated 9%. Common components:
explicit permitted uses 70%, disclosure/citation 63%, exam/quiz prohibitions 60% — and
**60% of policies had no enforcement mechanism** (reliance on trust over detection).
