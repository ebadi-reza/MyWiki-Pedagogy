# Self-Sufficiency

Pillar of the [[teaching-philosophy]].

[CLAIM] Self-sufficiency means students leave with the judgment and skills to solve
problems they have not seen before — built by developing confidence in their own
reasoning, exposing them to professional tools and methods, and teaching them to
evaluate and use resources critically. *(Source: [[candidate-statement]].)*

## How it shows up in courses

- **Critical adoption of professional tools.** [CLAIM] When students identified
  practical problems with SolidWorks and suggested web-based alternatives, Reza explored
  Onshape with them — treating the students' critical analysis of what they needed as a
  skill to foster, not a threat.
- **Live problem-solving.** In Statics, Kinematics, and Eng. Exp., problems and code are
  worked live — including dead ends, error, and recovery — so students witness the
  reasoning process and verification, not only polished outcomes. (Also a
  teaching-quality practice; see [[teaching-evaluations]].)
- **Open-ended community projects.** The [[kinematics-community-project]] has teams solve
  an unfamiliar, real-world problem (a mechanism to help a community) and explain it to a
  non-technical audience.

## Generative AI as an extension of the pillar

[CLAIM] The stance: uncritical AI use undermines self-sufficiency; refusing to engage
with AI leaves students unprepared. Both are avoided — "teach AI as a **tool, not a
crutch**" (see [[2025-ebadi-pbl]]).

- In **Eng. Exp. (ME 3902)**, students were permitted to use AI to generate the *syntax*
  for hardware-communication and data-collection code, but had to **demonstrate they
  understood the logic behind it**, assessed at **checkpoints throughout the term** (a
  sample "code-logic" exam that tests understanding without requiring detailed syntax is
  Appendix A.4 of the [[teaching-portfolio]]). [VALIDATED] Students performed well on the
  offline comprehension check, indicating AI served as an implementation tool, not a
  substitute for understanding.
- Also in Eng. Exp., the **final project requires each team to integrate at least one
  hardware component not covered in the course** — they must find the documentation,
  work out the communication protocol, and make it work on their own: self-sufficiency
  built directly into the assessment. [CLAIM]
- The **[[ai-verification-cycle]]** (introduced in Kinematics, C26) is the structured
  form of this: students solve independently → compare against AI-generated responses →
  join an instructor-led demonstration of domain-specific verification strategies. The
  aim is judgment about when AI is right, when it is wrong, and what to do about it.
  Participation is optional for students with principled objections. [CLAIM]

The AI-verification cycle is also the seed of the future [[ai-pedagogy]] research
program on AI verification and judgment. [ASPIRATIONAL]

## Project advising as the fullest expression
[CLAIM] Reza describes **[[project-advising]]** as where the self-sufficiency approach is
"most fully expressed": students own their MQP/IQP objectives while he provides
structure — 16 MQPs (64 students) and 6 IQPs (22 students) since 2021.
