## The AI Pedagogy Specialist role

[CLAIM] Reza was appointed **WPI's inaugural AI Pedagogy Specialist** (AY2025–26),
housed within the Morgan Teaching and Learning Center (MTLC). Concrete activities to date:
- **surveyed faculty and students** on their AI literacy and needs;
- **designed and ran workshops** on AI ethics and assignment/assessment redesign, so AI
  **scaffolds rather than replaces** student reasoning;
- helps develop AI-use policies;
- formed **student and faculty advisory circles**;
- runs the **AI Pedagogy Lab** — a monthly interactive session where faculty experiment
  with AI tools and explore pedagogical strategies (one session focused specifically on
  AI **ethics**);
- built and deployed the **AI Pedagogy website** ([aipedagogy.wpi.edu](https://aipedagogy.wpi.edu))
  to centralize resources and workshop materials.

[CLAIM] The role also surfaced an open challenge Reza names directly: how to expose
students to professionally relevant AI tools while honoring **principled ethical
objections** — which is why AI assignments are **optional**, not required. See the
student-objection design note in [[ai-verification-cycle]].

*(Sources: [[candidate-statement]], [[teaching-portfolio]], and the peer-reviewed
[[2026-ebadi-asee]].)*

## How the role is structured, and its first-year evidence
[VALIDATED] The 2026 ASEE paper ([[2026-ebadi-asee]]) reads the role through the **POD
Network's four CTL functions** (hub, incubator, temple, sieve) and shows it extending
three:
- **Sieve** — curating and promoting best practices (the website, a faculty listserv,
  research-grounded programming);
- **Incubator** — one-on-one coaching and an **Assignment Redesign Worksheet** for
  redesigning assignments/assessments;
- **Hub** — the **AI Pedagogy Labs** (monthly) and **Food for Thought** sessions
  (quarterly, faculty + students), plus an **AI Advisory Circle** and two student
  ambassadors.

[VALIDATED] **Inaugural-year reach:** needs-assessment surveys of **155 faculty** and
**107 students**; **69 faculty/staff** reached across four programming sessions; a
**43-policy** faculty AI-policy survey. Headline findings: **58%** of faculty use AI
mainly for their own work (not teaching); **ethics/integrity** was the top faculty need
(56%); **clear AI policies** the top student need (50%); and **60%** of faculty policies
had no enforcement mechanism. See [[ai-pedagogy-inaugural-year]].

## Stated framework for AI in courses
[CLAIM] In "Project-Based Learning for the Age of AI" ([[2025-ebadi-pbl]]), Reza lays out
a faculty-facing position: AI can remove the barriers (time, limited support) that keep
project-based learning rudimentary in 7-week terms, letting instructors **raise project
complexity** — but only if the **uneven AI readiness** across students is addressed, or
the added complexity **worsens equity** (an [[accessibility]] concern). His five
recommendations: start small and scaffold; **teach AI as a tool, not a crutch**; redesign
assessments around reasoning and **solution verification**; support hesitant students; and
**build faculty capacity**. The verification recommendation is realized in the
[[ai-verification-cycle]].

## Future research program

[ASPIRATIONAL] A research program on **AI verification and judgment in engineering
education** — its thesis stated crisply at the 2026 AI Summit ([[2026-ebadi-ai-summit]]):
**confidence ≠ correctness**, and **expertise cannot be outsourced**, so the aim is
graduates who reason *alongside* AI rather than defer to it. With the
[[ai-verification-cycle]] as its first step, Reza's own courses
(e.g. Kinematics, Eng. Exp.) as the laboratory, and the tools ([[gpthermo]],
[[studyguideai]]) as research platforms. Intended external funding: NSF IUSE, building
on lessons from prior submissions. A near-term step is a **"Summer Sandbox" proposal** to
formalize AI integration in Kinematics through weekly **"AI dissection"** sessions —
the same lineage as the C26 [[ai-verification-cycle]]. See [[scholarship-and-dissemination]].
