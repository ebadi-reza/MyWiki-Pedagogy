# Accessibility

Pillar of the [[teaching-philosophy]].

[CLAIM] Making robust engineering content available outside the classroom, and
providing cost-effective alternatives to expensive tools and equipment — so students
are not pushed toward unverified or erroneous free resources. *(Sources:
[[candidate-statement]], [[teaching-portfolio]].)*

> In the [[teaching-portfolio]], Reza treats **accessibility and [[self-sufficiency]]
> together**: the tools he builds for access are often the same ones that build
> independence — "if I want students to find resources and solve problems independently,
> I must lead the way in developing those resources." [CLAIM]

## Channels

- **Recorded lecture videos** in all courses, with viewing metrics tracked to identify
  areas for improvement. [CLAIM] These, along with posted lecture notes, trace to
  remote-teaching skills Reza built as a UNH lecturer during the pandemic and kept
  because students continued to find them useful (see [[covid-statement]]).
- **Optional virtual (hybrid) office hours** — a pandemic-era practice he retained:
  location-independent help, with screen-sharing for technical software such as
  SolidWorks. [CLAIM]
- **CAD tutorial/instructional videos** — short, marker-indexed clips so students can
  find specific content. [CLAIM]
- **[[note-templates]]** — lecture note templates that leave targeted blanks for
  students to complete during lecture, balancing engagement against the tedium of full
  note-taking. [CLAIM] Student feedback reported as overwhelmingly positive *(aggregate;
  see Teaching Portfolio when ingested)*.
- **Open Educational Resources (OER)** — a public CAD resource library shared with the
  WPI library, funded by a $3k EMPOwER grant from the WPI OER team. [VALIDATED — funded]
- **Cost-effective tool substitutes** — PMKS+ (an open-source, in-house mechanism
  simulator) replacing a former commercial package in Kinematics; promotion of
  web-based CAD (Onshape, Fusion 360) as alternatives to a cumbersome SolidWorks
  deployment. [CLAIM]
- **Low-cost lab hardware** — an affordable flow-visualization setup for Fluid Mechanics
  (ES 3004), Eng. Exp., and MQPs, developed on a $13k MTLC grant with a WPI colleague and
  undergraduate students. [VALIDATED — funded] It addressed a concrete gap Reza identified:
  there were **no flow-visualization experiments** in Fluids, Eng. Exp., or any listed
  MME/Aerospace MQPs for 2022–23. The design is a portable Particle Image Velocimetry
  (PIV) rig costing **≈$251 per unit** (vs. tens of thousands for a wind/water tunnel),
  paired with an open-source MATLAB/PIVlab app. [VALIDATED — built] It is a Work in
  Progress: efficacy is not yet measured (a SALG-survey assessment is planned
  [ASPIRATIONAL]). See [[2024-ebadi-asee]].
