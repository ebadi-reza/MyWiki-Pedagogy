# Stress Reduction

Pillar of the [[teaching-philosophy]]. The most evidence-rich thread in the wiki.

[CLAIM] The goal is to reduce unnecessary course and exam anxiety **while maintaining
rigor**, on the premise that anxiety consumes the cognitive bandwidth learning demands
— "remove the learning obstacles, not the challenge." *(Sources: [[candidate-statement]],
[[teaching-portfolio]].)*

[CLAIM] The work grew out of a growing awareness of **student mental-health challenges**
at WPI, which reshaped Reza's priorities toward protecting students' capacity to learn.

## The three core practices

Piloted in ES 3003 Heat Transfer (A22), then expanded to Kinematics (ME 3310; C23, C24,
C25) and Statics (ES 2501; B24), with survey data collected at each stage.

- **[[weekly-group-quizzes]]** — students collaborate for full participation credit.
  [VALIDATED] 77% of surveyed students agreed the quizzes deepened their understanding.
- **[[midterm-makeup-exams]]** — students reteach their lowest-scoring topic and earn a
  retake. [VALIDATED] 40.8% average improvement in exam performance (Kinematics).
- **[[two-part-final-exams]]** — students may revise Part 1 during Part 2.

See the consolidated figures in [[stress-reduction-survey-results]].

## External validation

[VALIDATED] An external evaluation by the Morgan Teaching and Learning Center (MTLC),
conducted in Reza's Kinematics course in C24, found that roughly one quarter of students
scored as highly-to-extremely stressed by typical academic activities, and that **92% of
those students reported at course end that each strategy worked** (moderate-to-very-high
positive impact). The same evaluation noted the practices did **not** reduce rigor. See
[[mtlc-external-evaluation]]. *(Reported in de-identified, aggregate form only; the
evaluator is not named — see the Publication rules in `CLAUDE.md`.)*

## Dissemination

[VALIDATED] A 2023 ASEE paper (co-authored with a WPI colleague) on the efficacy of
these modules has been downloaded more than once per day on average since publication.
Reza was subsequently invited to the Innovative Education Conference (University of
Rhode Island) to discuss combating exam stress, presenting a **second survey**
(Kinematics, n = 47, 100% participation) with module-by-module helpfulness and
learning-gain results. See [[scholarship-and-dissemination]] and the source pages
[[2023-ebadi-asee]] and [[2024-ebadi-iec]].
